Video Ads for SaaS: 3 Scripts and a 20-Second Framework

A SaaS video ad is a short promotion that shows a specific audience what your software helps them do. Start with one problem, show the relevant workflow or result, and end with one next step. A 20-second cut is a practical first version to test.

What should a SaaS video ad show?

Show the moment that explains why someone would use the product. For scheduling software, it might be a guest booking an available slot. For reporting software, it could be a dashboard bringing several metrics into one weekly view. Start with the action your buyer cares about and crop the screen around it.

Write the brief in one sentence before you edit: “Show [audience] how to [complete a task] so they can [get an outcome], then invite them to [take one action].” For example, show agency account managers how to assemble a client report from connected data, then invite them to view a sample.

How to storyboard a 20-second SaaS video ad

  1. 0–3 seconds · Hook. Name a task or frustration the buyer recognises. Put the hook on screen so it works with the sound off.
  2. 3–7 seconds · Product reveal. Show the product name and the relevant screen. Use a single caption to tell viewers what they are looking at.
  3. 7–14 seconds · Workflow. Show one action and its visible result. For a calendar app, tap an available slot and cut to the confirmation.
  4. 14–17 seconds · Outcome. Describe the benefit the viewer just saw, such as “Send one link and let guests choose a time.”
  5. 17–20 seconds · Call to action. Hold the brand and next step on screen long enough to read. Match the CTA to the destination, such as “View a sample report” if the link opens a report.

If the interface is too small to read on a phone, zoom into the control and its result. When the key workflow lives behind a login, use a recording from a demo account with sample data. A public landing-page capture works when the page itself shows the feature clearly.

Three SaaS video ad scripts you can adapt

These are illustrative scripts for hypothetical products. Replace the actions and calls to action with features and destinations your product actually offers.

1. B2B SaaS prospecting ad · Reporting software

Voice-over: “Still copying metrics into client reports every Friday? Connect your data, open the weekly report, and share one view with your client. See a sample report.”

On screen: Open spreadsheets, then cut to the software’s weekly report. Zoom into a chart and finish on a sample report preview.

2. App launch ad · Scheduling app

Voice-over: “Your next meeting can start with one link. Share your availability, let your guest pick a slot, and see the booking in your calendar. Create your booking link.”

On screen: Show the app name early, then the guest’s slot picker and the confirmed event. Keep the dates and tap target readable in the mobile crop.

3. Retargeting ad · Project management SaaS

Voice-over: “Planning a client kickoff? Open the project template, assign the first tasks, and share the board with your team. Preview the kickoff template.”

On screen: Start with the template open, show one assignment, then reveal the board. Link to the template preview.

How to choose the format, captions and footage

Design a vertical version when the intended placement fills a phone screen. Preview each export in the actual placement to see where buttons and labels cover the video. Use the guide to video ad formats and lengths to plan vertical, square and landscape versions.

Use captions to carry the hook and outcome through sound-off viewing. Keep important interface labels clear of captions, and choose stock footage only where it helps the viewer recognise the situation. For example, a desk with competing reports can establish context before a reporting demo. The product capture should supply the evidence for what the software does.

How to test a SaaS ad before scaling it

Make two versions with the same demo and CTA. Open one with the buyer’s problem and the other with the product in action. Keep audience, placement and destination as consistent as possible. Compare early viewing and clicks, then check what happens after the click. For a demo campaign, the relevant result is qualified demo bookings. For an app campaign, it might be trial starts or completion of the first setup step.

  • Can a new viewer identify the product and the task after one viewing?
  • Does the key interface action remain visible on a small screen?
  • Can you verify every rating, customer quote and performance claim shown?
  • Does the destination deliver the action named in the ad?

Frequently asked questions

How long should a SaaS video ad be?

Start with 15–30 seconds for a single workflow or launch message. Test a shorter cut when the audience already knows the product and a longer cut when the demo needs more context.

What is the difference between a SaaS ad and a product demo?

A SaaS ad earns attention and gives the viewer one next step. A demo shows how the software works in more depth. An ad can use a few seconds of demo footage to prove its main claim.
