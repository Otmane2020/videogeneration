Video ad script templates: six formulas for 15 to 30 second ads

The hardest part of a short ad is not the editing, it is deciding what to say in twenty seconds. These six structures give you a starting point you can fill in with your own product, whether you sell software or physical goods.

In shortA good 20-second video ad has four scenes of one short spoken sentence each (5 to 9 words), then a closing line with a clear button. Choose a structure first: product demo, problem and solution, feature showcase, startup launch, customer story or corporate, then write one idea per scene.

How long should a video ad script be?

For a 15 to 30 second ad, plan four scenes plus a closing screen. Each scene carries one spoken sentence of about 5 to 9 words and one on-screen headline of up to six words. That keeps the voice-over unhurried and the captions readable. Anything longer turns into a list of features.

Template 1: Problem and solution

Best when the audience feels a clear frustration. Scene 1 names the problem, scene 2 introduces the product, scene 3 shows the one capability that solves it, scene 4 states the outcome.

SaaS example: "Support tickets pile up." → "Meet an assistant that answers for you." → "It reads each question and replies in seconds." → "Your team finally catches up."

Template 2: Product demo

Best when people already know the category and need to see the product. Open on the product, then show one flow from start to result.

Example: "Here is your new dashboard." → "Connect your data in one click." → "See every sale as it happens." → "Start for free today."

Template 3: Feature showcase

Best for an update or one standout feature. Name the feature in scene 1, show how it works, give the benefit, then say who it is for. Resist adding a second feature: one ad, one idea.

Template 4: Startup launch

Best for new products. Lead with the promise, show the product, add the reason to believe, end with the invitation.

E-commerce example: "Meet the lightest runner we have made." → "Breathable knit, cushioned sole." → "Made for the daily mile." → "Shop the new release."

Template 5: Customer story

Best when you have a real customer result. Describe the situation, the change and the outcome. Use only facts and quotes you have published; never invent testimonials or figures.

Template 6: Corporate introduction

Best for brand awareness. Say who you are, who you serve, what makes you different, and where to find you. Keep the tone calm and the visuals clean.

How do you write a strong hook?

  • Name a frustration your customer had this week, not a general truth.
  • Pair it with footage of a person in that exact situation.
  • Keep it under seven words so the first caption lands in the first second.

How do you end the ad?

Close with the logo, the tagline and a button that says what happens next: "Start for free", "Book a demo" or "Shop now". "Learn more" is the weakest option.

Script checklist

  1. One idea per scene, five to nine spoken words.
  2. Every price, number and claim appears on your page.
  3. The first scene shows a person or the product, not a logo.
  4. Spoken numbers are written out in words so they are read correctly.
  5. The closing line names a clear action.

Frequently asked questions

How many scenes should a short video ad have?

Four scenes plus a closing screen is a good default for 15 to 30 seconds. Each scene carries one short spoken sentence.

What is the best video ad script structure?

It depends on the goal. Problem and solution works when customers feel a clear pain; a product demo works when they already know the category; a launch structure suits new products.

How many words should each line of voice-over have?

About 5 to 9 words. That keeps the pace natural and the captions easy to read.

Can I use customer testimonials in the script?

Only real ones you have published. Inventing quotes or figures damages trust and can break advertising rules.
